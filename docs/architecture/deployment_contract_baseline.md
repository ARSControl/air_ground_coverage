## MULLET integration facts

- `mullet_core` publishes `/mullet/heartbeat` (`DroneHeartbeat`, schema 3) at a configured rate, default 10 Hz, reliable, volatile, keep-last 5. It carries identity, state labels, capability flags, local pose validity and command/service paths. Local position is PX4 NED in metres, yaw clockwise from north, and each vehicle has its own local origin. Do not compare local coordinates across vehicles without registration. Heartbeat timestamps are snapshot times, not a proven sensor acquisition timestamp.
- Publish `MissionCommand` on each namespace's `command` topic. The subscriber is best-effort, volatile, keep-last 1. Modes include planar and 3D velocity, acceleration and trajectory. Planar velocity holds the altitude latched on entering MISSION and requires NaN z; NaN yaw faces horizontal velocity. Use explicit yaw when needed for cone orientation, and verify actual heading from telemetry.
- Commands are forwarded only in MISSION. The default command timeout is 0.25 s; stale commands hold position, and after the configured 2 s command-loss interval the supervisor enters HOLD. Source uses local receipt time for command freshness rather than the message stamp. Inspect deployed parameters rather than assuming defaults.
- Takeoff and landing are external to this repository and handled by MULLET's operational workflow. Start the initial coverage mission only after the drones are airborne, in HOLD, and verified at assigned altitude. Use heartbeat state and `can_*` flags and the advertised `start_mission` and `hold` paths for mission operation. Full mission-framework integration additionally publishes `MissionStatus` and serves `LoadMission` and `ListMissions`. Implement this full contract for operator-tool compatibility; validate exact lifecycle and QoS against the installed interface before coding.

## Proposed integration contract

- **Scenario:** immutable resolved YAML plus versioned assets and hashes; map coordinates in metres with x/y axes, origin, resolution and explicit per-vehicle map-to-local-NED rigid transforms. Register and test transforms in SITL and on hardware; do not infer identity transforms from ROS namespaces. Fail readiness on missing, inconsistent or unverified registration.
- **Truth and sampling:** a laptop scenario service owns static seeded LOW/HIGH fields initially, their declared relationship, and the virtual obstacle map. Requests carry scenario ID/hash, vehicle ID, role, sequence, acquisition time and map pose/heading. Responses carry accepted sample positions, values, fidelity, measurement times and request identity. Define per-vehicle seeded noise streams and reject duplicate or stale requests. HIGH cone samples obey range and virtual LOS; LOW footprint samples are unoccluded. No camera model is required.
- **Navigation:** distribute the same versioned obstacle geometry to ground-role agents. Ground-role commands must account for virtual obstacles; aerial-role commands ignore them. The current ground unicycle repulsion and segment guard are a baseline, not a drone safety guarantee. Evaluate a CBF before choosing a policy.
- **Estimator and planning:** one laptop process owns the central MFGP and shared coverage planning; agent processes perform command streaming and ground-role obstacle filtering locally. Pass timestamped observations to the estimator and versioned, timestamped planned commands or targets back to agents. Concrete topic names and message definitions remain to be designed and tested. Preserve the last valid posterior and expose update failures.
- **Time and startup:** use one configured time basis per run; log acquisition, receive and processing times separately, reject incompatible time bases and quantify delays. SITL clock propagation and real clock synchronization require empirical validation. Agents start in a non-commanding state, check scenario hash, valid pose, transforms and service health, and expose readiness; the operator initiates flight and mission. On stale plans or invalid state, stop producing motion and invoke the tested MULLET hold behavior. Verify this behavior before use; do not rely on an untested network-dependent fallback.

## Acceptance gates

1. Three SITL vehicles register to one map with measured position and heading errors within predeclared tolerances; frame tests include translation, rotation and sign conventions.
2. Agents enter and leave missions through MULLET, stream valid commands at a rate compatible with configured timeouts, and demonstrate observed hold on stale or invalid plans.
3. Ground-role samples obey cone and virtual LOS; aerial-role samples ignore occlusion. All components attest the same resolved scenario and map hash.
4. Ground-role trajectories maintain a predeclared virtual-obstacle clearance in deterministic SITL cases; log minimum clearance and infeasible/stalled cases. These results do not establish hardware safety.
5. A paired deterministic experiment shows that a ground-role HIGH observation changes the central HIGH posterior, aerial target density, and aerial command or trajectory. Archive configurations, seeds, logs and causal comparison.
6. Hardware operation remains gated on separate frame, timing, tracking, network, flight-area and safety validation.
